# A two-week trial

The aim is to find out whether the method saves you time, not to build the perfect system. Two weeks, Level 2. It works alone; it works better with two or three colleagues trying it at the same time and comparing notes.

## Before day 1 (30 minutes)

- Run the setup prompt, or follow INSTALL.md.
- Read SECURITY.md. Decide which sources the agent may read, and confirm that it drafts but does not send.
- Run the first "Start Active Work for today" and read the brief.

## The two weeks

- **Every working day:** start and close with the two phrases. Everything in between is normal work.
- **Day 3:** what's annoying? Usually the brief is too long or the agent asks too much. Both are one-line fixes in `AGENTS.md`.
- **Day 7:** read `carry-forward.md`. Is it the real list of your open work?
- **Day 10:** switch tool or model for a day. The day should carry on from the files without re-explaining anything. This is the real test.
- **Day 14:** review.

## How to judge it

1. Did you start and close on at least 8 of the 10 working days?
2. Is `carry-forward.md` a list you trust more than what's in your head or your inbox?
3. Did switching tool on day 10 work without re-explaining?
4. Roughly how much time did start and close take each day, and what did they save?
5. Keep, change, or stop?

Plus one number from the files: how many items were closed with a receipt over the two weeks.

If the answer is "stop", that is a fine result. Take what worked and use a method that fits you better.

## Known rough edges

- The first two or three briefs are generic until the list has real items in it. The questionnaire's last part exists to shorten this.
- Agents sometimes over-ask in the first days. Tell it "stop asking about X"; that becomes a standing rule.
- Daily files grow if long notes go in them. Notes belong in `work/`, with a one-line pointer.
- The close is the step people skip. If you miss it, the next start reconciles yesterday, but the record is thinner.
